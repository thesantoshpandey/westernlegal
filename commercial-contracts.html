Common questions

Contract FAQs.

What's the difference between the review and drafting fees?

A review takes your existing or incoming contract and returns a markup plus a written note of the risks and the changes to push for. Drafting starts from a blank page around your actual deal. Both are fixed-fee; a review can be upgraded to drafting with the review fee credited.

Can you draft terms & conditions for my website or SaaS product?

Yes, B2B and B2C terms, SaaS subscription terms, acceptable-use and SLA schedules, privacy and cookie policies. The practice ships its own software products, so SaaS terms are drafted by someone who has been on your side of them.

Do you do shareholder agreements?

Yes, including the articles amendments that make them work: leaver provisions, drag and tag, reserved matters, deadlock. £1,650 fixed for a standard two-to-four shareholder company.

My counterparty is overseas. Does that change anything?

It usually changes governing law, jurisdiction and enforcement strategy, which is exactly where a dual-qualified cross-border practice earns its fee. Cross-border contracts are a core lane here, not an exception.

Will you just tell me a contract is fine if it is?

Yes. A review that says 'sign it, here are the two clauses to watch in performance' is a good outcome, and you will not be upsold work you do not need.
