The heads

Heads of terms for a commercial lease: what to settle first.

The points below decide what the lease costs you over its life. Each is easier to win in the heads than in the lease, and much easier than in a dispute.

RENT

Rent and rent free

The rent, when it starts, any rent free period and what it is conditional on, and how often and on what basis the rent is reviewed.

TERM AND BREAK

Term and break

The length of the term and any break, with conditions you can actually meet: a break conditional on perfect compliance with every covenant is a break you may never be able to use.

REPAIR

Repair and condition

Whether you take the premises as they are and must hand them back in better repair. A schedule of condition annexed to the lease limits what you can be asked to put right at the end.

ALIENATION

Assignment and underletting

Whether you can assign, underlet or share occupation, and on what conditions, so the lease does not trap you if the business changes.

1954 ACT

Security of tenure

Whether the lease is protected by the 1954 Act or contracted out under section 38A, which decides whether you have a right to a new lease at the end.

MONEY

Costs, deposit and guarantee

Service charge, any rent deposit or personal guarantee, and who pays the landlord's legal costs.

Heads of terms are normally marked subject to contract, so neither side is bound by them. A binding contract to grant a lease has to be in writing, contain every term the parties have agreed and be signed by both (Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989, section 2); section 2 does not apply to a contract for a short lease of the kind in section 54(2) of the Law of Property Act 1925 (section 2(5)(a) of the 1989 Act). Agreement for lease

Agreement for lease solicitor: when the lease has to wait.

An agreement for lease binds both sides to grant and take the lease once conditions are met: the landlord's works, planning permission, a superior landlord's consent or the tenant's fit out. It is a contract for the disposition of an interest in land, so it must meet the same writing and signature rules (Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989, section 2).

We negotiate the conditions, the long stop date and what happens if a condition fails, and settle the agreement for lease to agreed form with the lease annexed to it. A new lease settled to agreed form is from £1,450; the whole transaction through to completion is from £2,450.

Common questions

Commercial lease negotiation: common questions.

Are heads of terms legally binding?

Normally not. They are marked subject to contract, and a contract to grant a lease binds only when it is in writing, contains every term the parties have agreed and is signed by both sides (Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989, section 2), except for a contract for a short lease of the kind in section 54(2) of the Law of Property Act 1925 (section 2(5)(a) of the 1989 Act). That is why the heads are the cheapest place to win a point: nothing is fixed yet.

The agent has agreed the heads. Do I still need a lease negotiation solicitor?

Agents agree the commercial headline: rent, term and incentives. The points that cost money later sit in the detail: break conditions, repair, service charge and who can assign. A review of the heads before you sign them is from £450.

What does it cost to negotiate a commercial lease?

Heads of terms drafted or reviewed from £450; a new lease or renewal settled to agreed form from £1,450; the whole transaction through to completion from £2,450. Each fee is fixed in writing before work starts, with no VAT.

Will my lease be protected by the 1954 Act?

A business tenancy for a term of years is protected unless it was contracted out before the term began, with the landlord's warning notice and the tenant's declaration done as the 2003 Order requires (Landlord and Tenant Act 1954, section 38A). The heads of terms should say which it will be, because it decides whether you have a right to renew.

Do you prepare the lease deed and register it?

We negotiate the terms and settle the documents to agreed form. Where the law requires a deed to be prepared or a Land Registry application made, an instructed authorised solicitor does that step.
