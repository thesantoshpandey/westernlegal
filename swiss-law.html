The basis on which we act

Advice on Swiss law, not representation in Swiss courts.

Santosh Pandey is a Solicitor of England and Wales, SRA No. 641612. He is not admitted to a Swiss bar, is not entered on a cantonal lawyers register and does not represent anyone before a Swiss court, conciliation authority or criminal authority.

Written advice on Swiss law outside court is not reserved to lawyers registered in Switzerland. The federal Lawyers Act applies to persons who represent parties before courts within the lawyers' monopoly (Art. 2 para. 1 BGFA), and the cantonal monopolies are drawn the same way, around civil and criminal proceedings (for example § 11 AnwG of the Canton of Zurich).

Where a matter needs a Swiss court, we say so and refer it to Swiss counsel, whose fee you agree with them before it is incurred.

Common questions

Swiss law opinion FAQs.

Can a solicitor of England and Wales advise on Swiss law?

Yes, outside court. Swiss law reserves the representation of parties before courts to registered lawyers (Art. 2 para. 1 BGFA; § 11 AnwG ZH); written advice on Swiss law is not reserved. We do not represent anyone before a Swiss court and never present ourselves as admitted in Switzerland.

Which Swiss law do you cover?

Swiss federal law, and the cantonal law of Zug, Zurich, Geneva, Vaud and Basel-Stadt. Other cantons are taken on request once the cantonal texts have been checked.

In what language is the opinion written?

English, with the original German or French text of each provision quoted beside it, so your Swiss counterparties can check the source.

Is the advice privileged in Swiss proceedings?

Not in the way a Swiss lawyer's advice is. In Swiss civil proceedings a party need not produce its correspondence with a lawyer entitled to represent parties professionally (Art. 160 para. 1 let. b ZPO). We are not entitled to represent parties before Swiss courts, so our correspondence does not have that protection. If a dispute is likely, tell us before you send documents.

What is not covered?

Representation before Swiss courts, conciliation authorities or criminal authorities; public deeds and other notarial acts; immigration; employment. We also do not take part in Swiss property purchases, or in setting up, running or funding non operating companies, which the Swiss Anti Money Laundering Act brings within its rules for advisers (Art. 2 para. 3bis GwG).
