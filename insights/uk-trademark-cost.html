How much does a UK trademark cost in 2026? The honest numbers.

Ask five providers what a UK trademark costs and you will get five structures designed to be hard to compare. Here are the real numbers, from a solicitor who publishes his fees.

The official fee everyone pays

The UKIPO charges an official filing fee for one class of goods or services, with an additional official fee per extra class. That fee goes to the government regardless of who files. Everything above it is professional cost, and that is where the market splits.

The three ways the market charges

Cheap filing websites charge little because they do little: no conflict search worth the name, no specification drafting, no one professionally accountable when an objection arrives. Traditional firms charge £1,500 to £3,500 on hourly rates, and the meter keeps running if anything goes wrong. This practice charges £555 all-inclusive for one class, official fee included, no VAT, with the specification drafted by a solicitor and a free conflict search before you commit. Additional classes are £245 each.

Why fixed beats hourly for trademarks

A trademark application is a definable piece of work with a definable risk profile. Hourly billing converts that certainty into your uncertainty. A fixed fee agreed in writing before payment converts it back: the price quoted is the price paid, and the incentive is to file it right the first time, within 48 hours of instruction, not to bill the file.

The costs nobody advertises

The expensive trademark is the cheap one that goes wrong: a weak specification, an undetected conflict, an opposition you face alone. Opposition defence properly done is a four-figure matter (ours is £1,200 fixed, and we have won at the Tribunal with costs). The £555 filing exists to make that day unlikely.
