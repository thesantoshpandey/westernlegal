Terminal dilapidations

Terminal dilapidations: what limits the claim.

A schedule of dilapidations lists the repair, reinstatement and decoration the landlord says the lease requires. Three things limit what a terminal dilapidations claim is actually worth.

SECTION 18 CAP

Section 18 of the 1927 Act

Damages for breach of a repairing covenant cannot exceed the amount by which the value of the landlord's reversion is diminished, and nothing is recoverable for failing to leave premises in repair if they are to be pulled down or so altered that the repairs would be valueless (Landlord and Tenant Act 1927, section 18(1)).

THE PROTOCOL

Schedule, demand, response

The Dilapidations Protocol expects the schedule generally within 56 days after the tenancy ends, the Quantified Demand within the same time, and the tenant's response usually within 56 days of the demand (paragraphs 3.3, 4.2.3 and 5.2).

THE EVIDENCE

Loss, not a price list

Before issuing proceedings the landlord is expected to quantify its loss with a formal diminution valuation, an account of what it actually spent, or both (Dilapidations Protocol, paragraph 9.1).

The covenants that matter are in the lease and any licences, so the review starts there; see also commercial lease review.

Common questions

Dilapidations Protocol and section 18 cap: FAQs.

When should the landlord send the schedule of dilapidations?

The Dilapidations Protocol says within a reasonable time, generally within 56 days after the tenancy ends (paragraph 3.3), with the Quantified Demand sent within the same timescale (paragraph 4.2.3).

How long does the tenant have to respond?

A reasonable time, which the Protocol says will usually be within 56 days after the landlord sends the Quantified Demand (paragraph 5.2).

Can the landlord claim the full cost of the works?

Not automatically. Section 18(1) of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1927 caps damages for breach of a repairing covenant at the diminution in the value of the reversion, and a claim for terminal repairs fails if the premises are to be pulled down or so altered that the repairs would be valueless.

What must the landlord produce before issuing a claim?

The Protocol expects a detailed breakdown of the issues and losses based on a formal diminution valuation, an account of actual expenditure, or both, unless that would be unreasonable (paragraph 9.1).

Is there a meeting?

The Protocol encourages the parties or their surveyors to meet on a without prejudice basis, generally within 28 days after the tenant's response (paragraph 7.1). Our own part is handled in writing.

Do you prepare the schedule or go to court?

No. Schedules and diminution valuations are surveyors' work, and any claim in court goes to instructed counsel or litigation solicitors. We check the schedule against the lease, advise on liability and the section 18 cap, and write the protocol correspondence.

Do we need to meet or call?

No. Everything is handled in writing, by email or WhatsApp: you send the documents, the fee is confirmed in writing, and the work comes back in writing. Replies within the hour in business hours.
