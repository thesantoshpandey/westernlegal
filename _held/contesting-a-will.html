Why this practice

The two kinds of challenge.

VALIDITY

Was it a valid will?

A party alleging the will was not duly executed, that the testator lacked capacity, or that it was obtained by undue influence or fraud must say so specifically, and a party alleging want of knowledge and approval must give particulars (CPR 57.7(3) and (4)). Section 9 of the Wills Act 1837 sets the signing and witnessing rules.

CAPACITY

The Banks v Goodfellow test

The testator must have understood the nature and effect of making a will, the extent of the property, and the claims they ought to consider. A duly executed, rational will is presumed to have been made with capacity until the objector raises a real doubt (Karim v Steele [2025] EWHC 2060 (Ch)).

PROVISION

Inheritance Act claims

A spouse or civil partner, some former spouses, a cohabitant of two years, a child, a person treated as a child of the family, or a dependant can apply for reasonable financial provision (section 1 of the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975), within six months of the first grant unless the court permits later (section 4).

Common questions

Contesting a will FAQs.

How long do I have to bring an Inheritance Act claim?

Six months from the date representation is first taken out, unless the court gives permission to apply later (section 4 of the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975). A claim can also be made before the grant.

Who can claim under the Inheritance Act?

A spouse or civil partner; a former spouse or civil partner who has not remarried or formed a new civil partnership; a person who lived with the deceased as a couple for the whole of the two years before the death; a child; a person treated as a child of the family; and anyone being maintained by the deceased immediately before the death (section 1).

What is a caveat?

A notice entered at a probate registry that stops a grant being sealed while it is in force. It lasts six months and can be extended (rule 44 of the Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1987). We advise whether one is justified; entering it is done by you or by an authorised firm.

The will was witnessed by video. Is it valid?

For wills made between 31 January 2020 and 31 January 2024, presence for witnessing includes presence by videoconference (section 9(2) of the Wills Act 1837). Outside that window the extended meaning does not apply.

Who has to prove capacity?

The person upholding the will. Where it was duly executed and looks rational, capacity is presumed, but once the objector raises a real doubt the burden returns to the person upholding it (Karim v Steele [2025] EWHC 2060 (Ch)).

Do you go to court?

No. This service is pre action only. Any claim is issued and run by instructed counsel or an authorised firm, and we prepare the file for that handover.
