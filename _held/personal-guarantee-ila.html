Common questions

Personal guarantee advice FAQs.

Why does the lender want a solicitor involved?

Where the relationship between the guarantor and the borrower is non commercial, such as spouses or partners, the lender is put on inquiry that the guarantor's agreement may have been obtained by undue influence. The steps it must then take include obtaining written confirmation from a solicitor acting for the guarantor that the nature of the transaction and its practical implications have been fully explained (Waller-Edwards v One Savings Bank plc [2025] UKSC 22, paras 32 and 33, summarising Royal Bank of Scotland plc v Etridge (No 2) [2001] UKHL 44).

What will the advice cover?

The House of Lords set a core minimum in Etridge (No 2), para 65: the nature of the documents and their practical consequences, including that you could lose your home or be made bankrupt; the seriousness of the risks, including the purpose, amount and terms of the facility and the amount of your liability; that the decision is yours alone; and whether you want to proceed or negotiate first (as set out in Padden v Bevan Ashford Solicitors [2011] EWCA Civ 1616, para 28).

Does the meeting have to be in person?

Etridge (No 2), para 66, says the solicitor's discussion with you should take place at a face to face meeting, in the absence of the borrower. We hold that meeting with you alone.

Can the borrower's solicitor advise me instead?

The case law allows the same solicitor in some cases if you are content (Waller-Edwards v One Savings Bank plc [2025] UKSC 22, para 33). We act only for the guarantor, never for the borrower or the lender, so the advice is independent of both.

What if the lender will not share the information?

Then no confirmation is given. Etridge (No 2), para 67, says the solicitor should obtain from the bank any information needed, and should decline to give the confirmation if the bank fails to provide it.

We are borrowing jointly. Does this apply to us?

It can. In Waller-Edwards v One Savings Bank plc [2025] UKSC 22 the Supreme Court held that, where the relationship is non commercial, a lender is put on inquiry if on the face of the transaction one party is standing surety for the other to more than a de minimis extent (paras 5 and 62).

Can I negotiate the guarantee?

Often. Etridge (No 2), para 65, contemplates negotiating with the bank, for example on the order in which the securities are called on or a specific or lower limit on your liability. We set out what is worth asking for before you sign.
