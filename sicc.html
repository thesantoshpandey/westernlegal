Who it is for

Who the SICC is for.

The SICC hears international and commercial disputes, international arbitration proceedings and cases transferred to it from the General Division of the High Court of Singapore.

Jurisdiction

What the SICC hears.

Written jurisdiction agreement

An action of an international and commercial nature that the General Division could hear at first instance, where each party has submitted to the SICC under a written jurisdiction agreement and no prerogative order is sought. Supreme Court of Judicature Act 1969, s 18D(1); SICC Rules O 2, r 1(1).

International arbitration

Proceedings relating to international commercial arbitration that the General Division may hear under the International Arbitration Act 1994. Section 18D(2)(a); SICC Rules O 23, r 3.

Transferred cases and pre action relief

Cases transferred from the General Division, and originating applications before proceedings for production of documents, an injunction or a search order. SICC Rules O 2, r 1(2).

International insolvency

Corporate insolvency, restructuring or dissolution proceedings that are international and commercial in nature. Section 18D(2)(c).

What counts as international and commercial

An action is international if a party's place of business, the place where a substantial part of the obligations is to be performed, or the place most closely connected with the subject matter is outside Singapore, or if all parties expressly agree that it relates to more than one State. It is commercial if it arises from a relationship of a commercial nature, contractual or not, or is an in personam intellectual property dispute (SICC Rules O 2, r 1(3)). The SICC may decline jurisdiction where hearing the case would be contrary to its international and commercial character (O 2, r 3(2)). We check this first, in the written claim review.

Rights of audience

Who acts for you before the SICC.

Every foreign lawyer representing a party in the SICC must be registered under section 36P of the Legal Profession Act 1966. A foreign lawyer granted full registration may represent a party in an offshore case, an action with no substantial connection with Singapore (SICC Rules O 3, r 3), and may give advice and prepare documents for it. A foreign lawyer granted restricted registration may only make submissions on foreign law, where the SICC permits.

Registration is generally valid for one year. The application is supported by a certificate of good standing issued no more than six months earlier, and the prescribed fee is S$300.

Common questions

SICC FAQs.

Can a foreign lawyer appear before the SICC?

Only if registered under section 36P of the Legal Profession Act 1966. A foreign lawyer with full registration may represent a party in an offshore case, one with no substantial connection with Singapore; restricted registration covers submissions on foreign law only.

Do both parties need to agree to the SICC?

For an action filed in the SICC, each party named when the case was first filed must have submitted to the SICC under a written jurisdiction agreement (SICC Rules O 2, r 1(1)). Cases can also be transferred from the General Division.
