The checklist

What goes in a shareholders agreement.

Six clauses decide almost every fallout between owners. If a template or an old agreement is silent on any of them, that is where the argument will start.

LEAVER

Leaver provisions

What happens to a shareholder's shares when they stop working in the business. Good and bad leaver definitions decide whether they are bought out at market value or at a lower price, and they should deal expressly with resignation, dismissal, long illness and death.

DRAG AND TAG

Drag along and tag along

Drag along lets an agreed majority make the others sell on the same terms when a buyer wants the whole company. Tag along lets a minority sell alongside when control is sold, instead of being left with an owner they did not choose.

RESERVED MATTERS

Decisions that need everyone

Issuing new shares, borrowing over a limit, changing the business, directors' pay and deals with connected parties can be made to need every shareholder, or an agreed majority, whatever the shareholding. For a minority this is the real protection.

DEADLOCK

How a deadlock ends

A 50/50 company can stall on every vote. Under the model articles for private companies the director chairing a board meeting has a casting vote on a tied decision (Companies (Model Articles) Regulations 2008, Schedule 1, article 13), which hands the board to whoever chairs. The agreement sets the way out: escalation, mediation, then an agreed buy and sell route.

PRE EMPTION

Who is offered shares first

On new shares, existing shareholders have a statutory right to be offered them first (Companies Act 2006, section 561), which a private company's articles can exclude (section 567). On a sale of existing shares there is no statutory right at all; the model articles only let the directors refuse to register a transfer (Schedule 1, article 26(5)). The agreement sets who is offered shares, at what price and how fast.

VALUATION

Valuation on exit

How a departing shareholder's shares are valued: the basis, whether a minority discount applies, who the valuer is and how one is appointed if you cannot agree. Leave it out and the argument moves from the price to who decides the price.

Without an agreement, a minority shareholder who is shut out is left with a petition to the court for unfair prejudice (Companies Act 2006, section 994), which is slow and costly next to a clause agreed in advance. The longer checklist is in our guide, what a shareholders agreement must contain; if there is already a dispute, see shareholder disputes.

Partnerships and LLPs

Partnership agreement solicitor: partnerships and LLPs.

A general partnership with nothing agreed runs on the default rules in the Partnership Act 1890: every partner shares equally in capital and profits (section 24(1)), no one joins as a partner without everyone's consent (section 24(7)), and where the partnership has no fixed term any partner can end it by notice (section 32(c)). An LLP without a members' agreement runs on similar default rules (Limited Liability Partnerships Regulations 2001, regulation 7), and no majority can expel a member unless the members have expressly agreed a power to do so (regulation 8).

Common questions

Shareholders agreement lawyer: common questions.

How much does a shareholders agreement cost?

From £1,650 for a standard private company, fixed in writing before any work begins, with no VAT. Anything beyond a standard private company is quoted in writing before work starts, and the fee does not change after you accept it.

Can I use a template instead of a shareholder agreement solicitor?

Many people start with one. A template cannot know your shareholdings, who works in the business, what each person put in or how you plan to exit, and the clauses that matter in a fallout (leaver terms, valuation and deadlock) are the ones a template leaves generic. Test any template against the six clauses above before anyone signs it.

Do we need one if we already have articles?

Usually, yes. Articles are filed at Companies House, where anyone can read them, and they bind the company and its members as its constitution (Companies Act 2006, section 33). A shareholders agreement stays private, binds each shareholder who signs it, and covers leaving, valuation, deadlock and exit in a way the model articles do not.

We are 50/50 shareholders and friends. Do we need one?

That is the structure that needs one most. With equal shareholdings neither of you can outvote the other at a general meeting, and nothing obliges either of you to buy the other out, so a falling out ends in stalemate or in court. Agreeing the exit while you are still friends is the cheap version of that conversation.
